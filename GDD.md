## Working title

Shifting Sands

![game cover](/references/cover.png)
## Game Summary

Spelunk your way through caves, recovering treasures, and using the environment to defeat your enemies!
Shifting Sands is a game where you have to problem-solve and strategically create cave-ins to crush your enemies and recover the hidden treasures.

## Inspirations

* I took a walk through the Robert M. Linsley Geology Museum once, and let's just say it rocked. The collection of various rocks, gems, and other geological artifacts was really impactful for me and one of my main inspirations for this game's theme.
![museum image](/references/linsley.png)
* The Powder Toy is a free sandbox physics game that I used to play a lot when I was younger, and it really inspired the idea of simulating the falling sand.
![powdertoy image](/references/powder_toy.png)
* The underground desert biome in the video game Terraria is one of the main aesthetic inspirations with regard to how the game would look, but I also want to mix in some more post-apocalyptic elements like ruins, abandoned buildings, etc.
![terraria undeground desert image](/references/terraria_underground_desert.png)
* The videogame Spelunky also served as inspiration to better understan the cave aesthetic, and how spelunking works in a videogame.

## Gameplay Overview

My main character doesn’t have a name; he is a roundish, short character with a spelunker hat and tan coloured clothes.

The player also has a companion, a small robot that is primarily formed by a crt, a big propeller, and some electronics hanging from the back.
![companion](/references/companion.png)
The idea is that this companion will stay at the surface and debrief the player on his objective, serve as a kind of tutorial to inform the player of basic controls, and to encourage the player.

The game is to adopt a cartoonish aesthetic with simple lines and bright colors.
The setting is primarily desert-themed. There is some small vegetation and metal scrap spread throughout the level, along with destroyed buildings in the background.
The buildings could be made to be inspired by the ones that are currently in Colgate, bringing the relationship between the game and the university closer, and hinting that we are situated at Colgate but in the far future, after humanity has fallen.

The main actions that the player can do are walk, jump, aim, and throw stones that they pick up from the ground. The stones that the player throws don’t damage the enemies directly, instead they affect the enviroment and are the main mechanism to create cave-ins.

Anything that is caught under a cave-in will be killed as rocks fall on top of it.
Cave-ins can also cause certain optional objectives to be obstructed, forcing the player to carefully plan how they are going to traverse the level.

Enemies are small bug-like robots that pace back and forth, damaging the player on contact with them. The player can take 3 hits from the enemies, but being caught under a cave-in will instantly kill them.
![enemies](/references/enemy.png)

There is also dynamite spread throughout the level. It explodes in a circle, instantly killing whoever is caught inside it, but it also destroys the sand when it explodes. It only gets activated if it is hit directly with a rock,
and it is meant to be a double edged sword, giving more choices to the player.

This game is one of careful reading of the environment and planning. I want the player to stop and consider multiple paths, and that they try to think ahead for what is goin to happen after a cave-in.
They also have to carefully read the environment to identify what part of it could be at risk of a cave-in if they were to throw a stone.

## Theme Interpretation

The player has been tasked with finding and recovering these lost artifacts from the Geology Museum, as in the future they are very valuable. The companion serves as a sort of cheerful but insistent character like Navi in the Zelda videogames, that is very adamant on having the player retrieve these artifacts at any cost.

My game doesn’t have that much by way of explicit exposition to the player or the main character. The different elements, like the Colgate buildings, rocks from the museum, etc, are meant to be understood
by someone who is familiar with the Colgate enviroment, and these are subtle nods to the setting of the game.

The idea is that the atmosphere is cartoonish in nature, as all the characters act and talk in a carefree way, but contrasting that with the seriousness of humanity being destroyed after some event is important to me.
I want the player to always be wondering exactly what event could have happened to lead to this event, while also being engaged by the gameplay and getting through the levels. The idea is to achieve this by
Having the primary assets that the player interacts with be simplistic, while the stuff that is in the background or that the player can’t interact with can be more detailed.

The main character also won’t have any dialogue, as building on a story besides a simple premise falls outside the scope that I previewed for this platformer.

**Videogame story**

1. The game would start by the main character sleeping peacefully in their camp; this serves to establish a reasonable starting point and to quickly understand what the stakes are for our main character in this video game.
2. Then the companion character would wake up the main character and promise them lots of riches if they just helped them to retrieve these artifacts; this is the call to action, serving to motivate the main character.
3. The idea then is that the player would go through the levels, and that at the last artifact that they recover would be the coveted dinosaur egg, causing the companion character to thank them and give them the riches that they promised, resolving the story.

## Mechanics

* Jumping: A basic jump used to traverse the level.
* Walking: The player walks left to right, but if they walk over a weak part of the level, that can cause a cave-in.
* Throwing rocks: The player first aims and then throws a rock a certain distance.
* Cave-ins: A part of the enviroment suddenly starts falling, following gravity and crushing anyone that is in its way.
* Mining: The player can mine a certain part of the enviroment to get through to the other side.

**Secondary Mechanics**
* Dynamite: A stick of dynamite can be activated by throwing a rock, exploding anything near it, and also causing cave-ins.
* Secondary objectives: Another artifact that is hidden or out of the way, and that requires more effort by the player to get it

## Types of surfaces and objects 
![level](/references/level.png)

* Hard Rock: This type of terrain is solid and can’t be mined.
* Mineable Rock: This terrain can be mined, and it's solid.
* Loose rock: This terrain is susceptible to cave-ins, meaning that any disturbance will cause it to start flowing like sand.
* Decoration: Can’t be interacted with in any meaningful way.
