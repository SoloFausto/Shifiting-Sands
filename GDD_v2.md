## Game Summary
![Game Screenshot](/screenshots/screenshot_1.png)
Spelunk your way through caves, recovering treasures, and using the environment to defeat your enemies!
Shifting Sands is a game where you have to problem-solve and strategically create cave-ins to crush your enemies and recover the hidden treasures.

## Gameplay Overview

My main character doesn’t have a name; he is a roundish, short character with a spelunker hat and tan coloured clothes.
<img src="/assets/idle.png" alt="main character" width="200" style="image-rendering: pixelated;"/>


The player also has a companion, a small robot that is primarily formed by a crt, a big propeller, and some electronics hanging from the back.
<img src="/assets/companion.png" alt="companion" width="200" style="image-rendering: pixelated;"/>

The idea is that this companion will debrief the player on his objective, serve as a kind of tutorial to inform the player of basic controls, and to encourage the player in their mission.

The game is to adopt a cartoonish aesthetic with simple lines and bright colors.
The setting is primarily desert-themed. There are different types of rocks spread throughout the level, along with some distant destroyed buildings in the background.

The main actions that the player can do are walk, jump, aim, and throw stones that they pick up from the ground. The stones that the player throws don’t damage the enemies directly, instead they affect the enviroment and are the main mechanism to create cave-ins.

Anything that is caught under a cave-in will be killed as rocks fall on top of it.
Cave-ins can also cause certain optional objectives to be obstructed, forcing the player to carefully plan how they are going to traverse the level.

Enemies are small bug-like robots that pace back and forth, damaging the player on contact with them.

<img src="/references/enemy_single.png" alt="enemy" width="200" style="image-rendering: pixelated;"/>


There is also dynamite spread throughout the level. It explodes in a circle, instantly killing whoever is caught inside it, but it also destroys the sand when it explodes. It only gets activated if it is hit directly with a rock,
and it is meant to be a double edged sword, giving more choices to the player.
<img src="/assets/dynamite.png" alt="dynamite" width="200" style="image-rendering: pixelated;"/>


This game is one of careful reading of the environment and planning. I want the player to stop and consider multiple paths, and that they try to think ahead for what is goin to happen after a cave-in.
They also have to carefully read the environment to identify what part of it could be at risk of a cave-in if they were to throw a stone.

## Theme Interpretation

The player has been tasked with finding and recovering these lost artifacts from the Geology Museum, as in the future they are very valuable. The companion serves as a sort of cheerful but insistent character like Navi in the Zelda videogames, that is very adamant on having the player retrieve these artifacts at any cost.

My game doesn’t have that much by way of explicit exposition to the player or the main character. The dinosaur egg is meant to be understood by someone who is familiar with the Colgate enviroment, and these are subtle nods to the setting of the game.

The idea is that the atmosphere is cartoonish in nature, as all the characters act and talk in a carefree way, but contrasting that with the seriousness of humanity being destroyed after some event is important to me.
I want the player to always be wondering exactly what event could have happened to lead to this event, while also being engaged by the gameplay and getting through the levels. The idea is to achieve this by
Having the primary assets that the player interacts with be simplistic, while the stuff that is in the background or that the player can’t interact with can be more detailed.
<img src="/assets/bg.png" alt="dynamite"  style="image-rendering: pixelated;"/>


The main character also won’t have any dialogue, as building on a story besides a simple premise falls outside the scope that I previewed for this platformer.

**Videogame story**

1. The game would start by the main character sleeping peacefully in their camp; this serves to establish a reasonable starting point and to quickly understand what the stakes are for our main character in this video game.
2. Then the companion character would wake up the main character and ask them for their help in retrieving these gems; this is the call to action, serving to motivate the main character.
3. The idea then is that the player would go through the levels, and that at the last artifact that they recover would be the coveted dinosaur egg, finishing the game.

## Technical Section
### Falling sand simulation
One of the most important technical aspects of my game is the simulation of falling sand. To do this, I used celular automation to enforce some basic rules to each particle that made it behave like a grain of sand.

At the most basic level, the game consists of a grid of blocks that each can be one of the types described above. To make the grains actually feel like grains of sand, I created a grid that is five times as smaller than the regular level grid to simulate the grains of sand there. Visually, I subdivide each grain into four distinct squares to make it more visually appealing and actually look like sand without sacrificing performance.

The basic rules for each grain of sand are:
- If there is air below the grain of sand, then it should move to that tile
- If there grain below is occupied, but there is space adjacent to it, then it should move to that tile
- If none of those rules are true, then the grain should stay put.

The mineable rock works incredibly similarly to the rock, as I also subdivide the game grid and add the stone grains, but they remain static and only dissapear when activated.

### Stone throwing, mining, and sand clump activation
The other component of the main aspect of my game is throwing rocks to cause these sand clumps be simulated with the rules above. The exact behavior that I wanted was that the sand would be static in the enviroment, and when the player threw a rock or did another action that affected any sand grain, suddenly all the sand that was in this clump would start falling. To achieve this, each sand grain has a boolean called "active" that determines if it should be simulated with the rules described above, or if it should just remain static. 

When there is an action that happens to collide with a grain of sand, this grain of sand is added to a list to be activated. For each grain that is in this list, we set this boolean to be active, and then look for its neighbours and add them to the list to be activated. This makes all the grains in this sand clump be activated and simulated. 

The mineable rock also has an active boolean, but this one makes each grain dissapear and not interact anymore with the world. When the character "mines" it actually throws an invisible rock that has a very limited range; and when this rock collides with a grain, all the grains that are within a distance from this grain as active, causing that small area to dissapear or to be "mined."

## Art Section

The art in this game was done with a mix of generative AI tools and manual art.

For the image generation, **Gemini and ChatGPT's image generation** was used to generate the images with prompts like:
"Create a pixel-art 32x32 style roundish main character for a platformer videogame, the character is a male spelunker with a white explorer outfit."

or taking advantage of the tool's multimodal capabilities to feed it images and have them be remixed with prompts like:
"Remake this enemy critter robot as a 32x32 sprite with a red antenna and eyes, and that it is slightly rusty and made from scrap."

After the images where generated, then I used **Gimp** to first convert the high resoulution images into actual pixel art by a process of downscaling the image's resolution, and then manually corrected the images to fix the inherent inconsistencies that image generation has.

Another tool that was used in a lesser degree was **Material Maker**, as it is very good at creating procedural materials that can be quickly modified.
